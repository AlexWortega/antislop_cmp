Earlier prompts all aimed at "casual forum typing" through lists of tics: lowercase letters, comma splices, banned words. That approach stayed at about 4% human, and every output still read as a tidy explainer that walks a mechanism step by step and talks to "you". The only texts that passed were rewrites of human source text. The target examples are also not chatty. They are dry, declarative and situated: a review, a mailing-list post, an agency memo, a complaint.

This version changes the generative frame instead of patching surface features. The model writes as a specific kind of document writer, an old mailing-list or Usenet regular, or someone writing a review or an office memo. That writer states conclusions flatly and uses field terms without defining them. Their claims are narrower and more hedged, and they leave out the scaffolding of didactic explanation: analogies addressed to "you", "this means", step chains. The prompt targets the discourse features a detector picks up on: explanation cadence, symmetric coverage and the reader-coaching stance. It drops character-level tricks.

Reasoning stays to a few notes, and temperature is raised slightly to make word choices less predictable.
