- They open by restating the topic as the grammatical subject ("Carbon monoxide is dangerous mainly because...", "When an aircraft is in an emergency...").
- They chain one mechanism after another.
- Their sentences are uniformly polished.

The texts the real Pangram passed read like reviews or work emails. They state a stance, give one concrete case, and use specific nouns and plain declarative claims. They do not explain processes.

This edit makes four changes:
- **Opening:** add an explicit rule against opening with the question's topic as the subject. The first sentence should instead react to the person's situation or give a direct verdict.
- **Content cap:** limit explanations to one reason plus a practical consequence or opinion.
- **Note recasting:** tell the model to treat notes and explanations as "what I'd tell a coworker who asked", using a verdict, then the reason, then what to do. This avoids the textbook layout.
- **Sampling:** raise temperature slightly (1.05 to 1.15) and top_p (0.92 to 0.96) to raise token-level perplexity. Task score has headroom (8.44), so this risk is acceptable.

The reasoning stays brief so outputs don't come back empty.
