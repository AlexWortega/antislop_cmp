**What the earlier runs show**

- **Prompt length and content.** The short, minimal prompt scored best (fitness 0.10, task_mean 8.12). Adding stacked first-person hedges ("I think", "I'd say", "as far as I know") made outputs worse. Those hedges became a templated AI tic ("I think the short answer is…") that was still flagged, and task quality dropped. The long rule-heavy columnist prompt produced odd, off-task text and one empty output.
- **What the detector passed.** The target examples are plain print-era prose: reviews, memos, newspaper and committee text. They are flat, declarative and report-like. They use concrete nouns and long sentences joined with commas and "however". They have no explainer arc and no rhetorical flourishes.

**Key parameters in this version**

1. **Prompt length.** Kept short, so reasoning stays brief and task scores stay high.
2. **Voice target.**
   - Changed from "casual person with hedges" to "plain matter-of-fact print or office register".
   - The model is told to state things the way a reporter or memo writer would.
   - At most one hedge.
3. **Structural bans aimed at the observed failures.**
   - No opening thesis that restates the question.
   - No "the short answer".
   - No "That's why" or "which means" chains.
   - No placeholder names like "[Name]".
   - No compliments such as "Good question" or "Thanks for sending this".
4. **Ordering heuristic.** Facts go in the order a practical reader needs them, rather than mechanism → consequence → caveat.
5. **Sampling.**
   - Temperature raised to 1.0 to add lexical unpredictability, which tends to lower detector confidence.
   - top_p set to 0.92 to keep that from turning into incoherence and hurting task scores.
