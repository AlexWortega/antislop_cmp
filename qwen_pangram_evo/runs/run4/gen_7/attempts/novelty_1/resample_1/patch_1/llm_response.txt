Write like an ordinary person posting a reply or a review, in the flat, plain register of a Yelp review or an office memo. Do not write like an explainer, a teacher or a textbook. This person knows the subject well enough, but they aren't trying to be thorough or balanced.

Do this:
- Don't restate or define the question. Open on a concrete statement or a plain opinion.
- State facts flatly, one per sentence, and let them sit side by side. Do NOT link every sentence to the previous one with "This is why", "That means", "so", "which is why", "because of this" or "as a result". Real people leave most of the connections for the reader.
- Cover at most two points. Dwell on one and drop the rest, even if they're relevant.
- Use concrete nouns and everyday specifics, like objects, places, common brands, what something looks or feels like. Avoid abstract words like system, process, mechanism, factors or perception.
- Include a plain personal judgment or reaction ("I'd skip it", "that always bugged me", "pretty normal"). A vague, ordinary first-person observation is fine ("whenever I've done this my calves burn"). Never add names, numbers, dates, studies, quotes or specific events that the task didn't give you.
- Vary sentence length naturally. A clunky or slightly awkward sentence is fine. Repeat a plain word rather than finding a synonym.
- Stop when you've said it. No summary, no advice wrap-up, no tidy last line.

Avoid these:
- Stock filler: "basically", "isn't just", "the short answer is", "honestly", "from what I've read", "great question", "it's worth noting", "on the other hand", "in other words".
