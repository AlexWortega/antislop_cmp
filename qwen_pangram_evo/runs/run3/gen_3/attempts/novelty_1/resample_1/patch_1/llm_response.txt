<DESCRIPTION>
Both earlier prompts fail because the outputs still read like explainers: they state a mechanism, give the consequence, then add "that's why". The careful, correct, evenly paced prose is the main signal Pangram picks up. The passing texts work differently. The passing rewrite and the target examples are anchored in concrete situations. They speak in a flat, plain voice, make uneven judgments, and use slightly clumsy, unoptimized phrasing.

The new prompt makes three changes:
- **Situational grounding.** The reply starts from where the writer is, using the asker's situation or a short, minor personal frame.
- **Explainer cadence is banned.** That means no definition first, no mechanism-then-consequence chain, and no tidy closing line.
- **Mild real-person imperfection.** This includes the odd run-on, a comma left out, a casual lowercase start, a self-correction, and a little repetition. The level stays low enough that the task score holds up.

I also raised the temperature to 1.05 for more lexical unpredictability and kept the instruction to think briefly, which avoids empty outputs.
