<DESCRIPTION>
The sample-based prompt is the only one that produced a passing output, but 8 of 25 tasks came back empty. Each empty one used the whole 4096-token budget on reasoning, which also pulled down the length and task scores.

The two earlier prompts had a firmer thinking limit and almost no empty outputs. This edit replaces the one-line budget with that stronger limit, with these additions:
- It explicitly forbids checking the draft against the samples or the NEVER list while reasoning. Self-auditing against a long ban list is the likely cause of the runaway reasoning.
- It says a missed rule is much cheaper than an empty answer.

It also reframes the style rules as habits rather than a checklist the model must verify. The last change adds one human-likeness cue: prefer concrete, specific everyday details over abstract explanation, since abstract explanation dominated the flagged outputs. That is still bounded by the rule against invented facts.
