**What the failures showed**
- **Empty outputs grew with prompt length.** Empty outputs (budget burned on reasoning) went from 3–4 with short prompts to 8 with the long checklist prompt. Long rule lists make the model audit its draft against every rule while thinking.
- **The only pass was a rewrite.** It kept the source's original human voice almost untouched.
- **Fluent explanation gets flagged.** Every flagged answer was a tidy, evenly paced explainer, even when it was sprinkled with "honestly".

**The new approach**
1. **One short persona instead of a checklist.** There are fewer items to verify, so the model has less to re-check while thinking.
2. **An explicit thinking cap with a reason.** The prompt says rules are not to be checked one by one, and that the first natural wording is the wanted output.
3. **Rewrites as minimal-edit copy-editing.** The model keeps most of the source's own sentences, quirks and order, and only smooths the wording where asked. This preserves the human signal the detector rewards and also stops sentence-by-sentence planning.
4. **Answers, notes and posts in a real forum-comment register.** That means:
   - a blunt opinion first;
   - uneven, slightly messy sentences, with an occasional lowercase casual fragment;
   - talking about one angle rather than an ordered tour of the mechanism;
   - comparing with what "most people" think;
   - mild uncertainty.
5. **No invention of facts.** The prompt still forbids made-up facts and keeps length targets.
