**What the current prompt controls.** It has three levers:
1. A loose instruction about how much to think.
2. Abstract style advice ("plain words, uneven sentences").
3. A short ban list.

**What went wrong.**
- **Overthinking.** Three rewrites came back empty because reasoning used the whole budget. "Think briefly" is too vague for this model.
- **Wrong register.** Almost every other output was flagged as AI at scores of 92 to 97. They read like textbook explanations: a definitional opener ("X is a…", "When you…"), then a tidy chain of mechanisms, in a neutral, impersonal voice.
- **Off-task answers.** One email failed by announcing missing information and answering generic questions instead. One answer opened by disputing the question's premise.

**What the new prompt changes.**
- **Hard thinking cap.** Reasoning is limited to about 5 short lines. It forbids drafting, counting words or re-checking in the reasoning. For rewrites, it says to start writing almost immediately.
- **A concrete persona.** The voice is a knowledgeable person typing a reply, not an explainer. That means:
  - first person and contractions;
  - mild opinion and hedging ("I think", "honestly", "pretty sure");
  - a parenthetical aside;
  - mixed sentence lengths;
  - a slightly lopsided structure;
  - opening on the direct answer or a reaction, never a definition.
- **Bigger ban list.** It now covers specific AI-marker words and the "mechanism chain" structure.
- **No fake experience.** Personal stance is allowed (opinions, uncertainty), but invented anecdotes and numbers are not. This keeps "no invented facts" intact.
- **Task handling.**
  - Work with whatever information is given, without meta comments.
  - Answer the question as asked.
  - For rewrites, change rhythm and voice while keeping every fact.
- **Length.** Hit roughly the requested length, without counting words during reasoning.

**Expected effect.** Fewer empty outputs, a large jump in the human-likelihood score, and preserved task scores.
