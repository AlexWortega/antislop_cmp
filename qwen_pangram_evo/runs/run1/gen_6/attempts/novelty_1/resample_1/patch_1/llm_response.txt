<DESCRIPTION>
The earlier versions were rule-driven. They stacked long lists of banned words and patterns. The model mostly ignored the voice rules and still wrote fluent textbook explanations, especially in notes. The heavy casual and lowercase style also cost task score without fooling the detector.

This version is example-driven instead. It gives a few short, original human-style samples on unrelated topics, one per kind of writing. The model is told to copy their rhythm and density, never their content. Models imitate concrete examples much more reliably than they follow abstract rules.

It also adds a small generation procedure. The model picks one angle, opens with a reaction or the answer, gives one judgement call, and stops early. This avoids the full-coverage explainer structure that detectors key on.

Reasoning is capped at a two-line plan, and rewrites start immediately, to get rid of empty outputs. The task rules target the observed off-task failures:
- Answer within the asker's frame.
- Never fabricate or point out missing questions.
- Rewrites keep all facts.
</DESCRIPTION>

Heat reverses it for a while. Toast a stale slice and the inside goes soft again, at least until it cools. Splashing the crust with water and giving it five minutes in a hot oven works better than it has any right to.

- At least one judgement call or opinion ("worst place", "works better than it has any right to", "don't bother").

- Normal capitalization and punctuation, contractions throughout. Not sloppy, just relaxed.

- Lists of three.

- Post: a take on the topic in your own voice, without a made-up personal story.
- Rewrite: keep every fact, name and number from the source and add nothing new. Reorder and reshape the sentences so it reads like a person retelling it, and keep roughly the source's length.
