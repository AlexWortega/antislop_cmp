Quick take on *Awakening* at Wynn Las Vegas: it’s fine, but not the Vegas must-see I’d build a trip around.

I’ve been taken to Las Vegas constantly since I was a kid—saw a ton of shows before I turned 21—so I’ve got a decent baseline for what these productions can be.

Good news: the circular stage is genuinely smart. From basically any seat, you get a decent view, which is rare in these big theater shows. If you’re staying at Wynn or Encore, there’s a solid deal: tickets normally $125 are $99 for hotel guests. That helps.

Some individual acts are impressive, and the performers are clearly strong as hell—aerialists, acrobats, whatever. But a lot of the feats feel like things I’ve seen done just as well in Cirque du Soleil shows.

The bigger problem is the story. It’s unclear, weak, or practically nonexistent; I left not really knowing what the production was trying to say. There’s also too much going on at once—too many visuals, too many moving pieces, too many “wow” attempts. A more restrained production would hit harder.

Aside from a few cool stunts, the whole thing felt average. I wasn’t blown away, and no one I was with seemed overwhelmed either. A few friends did enjoy it, so if you’ve never seen it, maybe check it out—especially with the Wynn/Encore deal. But I wouldn’t see it again or pay full price a second time. It tries to be the stereotypical huge, flashy Vegas spectacle where more equals better. More just meant messier for me.
