Remote work is heralded as the panacea. From my two years of mentoring 5 junior engineers, it's overblown for any juniors still learning how to code.

Jrs don't need tickets or documentation or meeting. They need to sit in on design conversations. They need to see how others debug issues. They need to ask questions before they have hours of confusion. That kind of interaction happens organically in an office. For remote work, they need to know the right times to cut in, set up a call, and to explain a problem that they might not even fully understand yet. It's a skill (and a tough one!) to have before you have a chance to learn it.

I've seen the feedback issues in remote work. Short comments on code review can feel like the final word instead of a learning opportunity. Also, it takes longer, so less momentum. The jrs I mentored typically improved faster if they had in person pairing, whiteboard sessions, etc. and had easy access to seniors.

I'm not trying to push for offices or anti-flexibility. Remote work can be great for self-driven devs. But for jrs, proximity is a booster that gives confidence, context, and instincts. Sending jrs off with a laptop and a calendar can be a drag on junior development which can result in less good technical leadership in the future.
