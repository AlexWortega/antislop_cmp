A float valve connects the trough to a pressurized water line. A buoyant float, usually a plastic ball or block, rests on the water surface and is attached to a lever arm. When cattle drink and the level drops, the float sinks, tilting the arm so it pulls a plunger or washer away from the valve seat. Water then flows into the trough.

As the water rises, the float lifts with it. The lever pushes the plunger back against the seat, gradually narrowing the opening until flow stops at the set level.

Overflow commonly happens when a worn or debris-fouled washer cannot seal against the seat. It also occurs when the float is punctured and waterlogged, so it no longer rises enough to close the valve.
