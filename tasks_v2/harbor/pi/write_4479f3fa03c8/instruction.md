Old school attendance registers can help historians reconstruct patterns of daily life that are often missing from official narratives. They show which children were present or absent, how regularly they attended, and sometimes why they missed school. From this, historians can infer seasonal labor demands, illness, bad weather, poverty, family responsibilities, migration, or local emergencies. Registers may also reveal differences by gender, age, neighborhood, or social class, helping researchers understand how education fitted into work, household routines, and community expectations. When studied across many years, they can show changes in schooling habits and public health.

A limitation is that attendance registers record only what the school chose to note. They rarely explain children’s experiences in detail, and absences may be misreported, incomplete, or shaped by teachers’ assumptions. Therefore, they are most useful when compared with other sources.
